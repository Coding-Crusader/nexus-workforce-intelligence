==================================================
1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
==================================================

Conventional talent matching systems (such as job boards and static resume matchers) operate on a descriptive paradigm: they measure where a candidate currently fits based on keyword overlap or similarity scoring. This approach introduces two critical shortcomings in workforce mobility:
1. Static Trap: Candidates are informed only of roles they already qualify for, reinforcing career inertia and failing to expose reachable adjacent career trajectories.
2. Unprioritized Gap Guidance: When skill gaps are identified, candidates receive undifferentiated advice (e.g., "learn everything you lack") without quantifying which specific capability enhancement unlocks the greatest expansion in accessible opportunities.

In the context of the SAS Hackathon (centered on data science jobs, analytical skills, and career progression outcomes), the problem is to construct an evidence-grounded, decision-support platform that moves beyond static matching. NEXUS solves this by framing workforce transition as an empirical, counterfactual optimization problem: given a candidate's verified capability state and actual labor-market demand, which targeted intervention produces the highest leverage in expanding reachable opportunities?

==================================================
2. ANALYTICS OBJECTIVE
==================================================

The primary analytics objective of NEXUS is to build a deterministic Workforce Transition Intelligence engine that maps empirical labor market demand, evaluates multi-dimensional candidate-role compatibility, identifies capability bottlenecks, and simulates the counterfactual impact of targeted upskilling interventions.

Decision Supported:
The system supports the strategic reskilling decision for candidates, educational mentors, and workforce planners: "Among all potential skills or certifications, which specific capability investment maximizes career mobility and market access?"

The Central Counterfactual Question Answered:
"If a candidate improves capability dimension X by delta Y through intervention I, how does their opportunity landscape change in terms of reachable roles, accessible market vacancies, and benchmark salary potential?"

5. High-Performance Skill Normalization:
   - 79,880 raw skill mentions across 8,535 unique terms are mapped via a pre-compiled inverted dictionary lookup ("backend/data_cleaner.py") to 10 canonical skill keys in O(1) time per token.

6. FEATURE ENGINEERING / DERIVATION
==================================================

1. Normalized Canonical Skills:
   - Aggregated raw multi-token strings into 10 standardized binary indicators per job posting using "config/skill_aliases.json".

2. Role Skill Demand Intensity Matrix (R_jk):
   - For each standardized role family j and skill k, computed proportion of postings requiring skill k:
     P_jk = Count(Jobs in role j requiring skill k) / Total Jobs in role j
   - Scaled relative to the dominant core skill in that role:
     R_jk = P_jk / max_k(P_jk) (range 0.0 to 1.0).

3. Candidate Capability State (C_k):
   - Vector in [0.0, 1.0]^10 representing candidate proficiency across canonical skills.

4. Evidence Confidence Score with Skill Half-Life:
   - Implemented from presentation Slide 04:
     Confidence_k = BaseScore_k × StrengthMultiplier × 2^(-RecencyMonths / T_half)
     where StrengthMultiplier ∈ {STRONG: 1.00, MEDIUM: 0.85, SUPPORTING: 0.75, WEAK: 0.60} and T_half = 18.0 months.

5. Role Compatibility Score:
   - Multi-component linear combination with core competency penalty (see Section 8).

6. Bottleneck Priority Score:
   - Composite metric ranking capability gaps by transition leverage (see Section 10).

7. Opportunity Expansion Ratio:
   - Quantified relative expansion in reachable role count (see Section 11).

8. MARKET / OPPORTUNITY INTELLIGENCE
==================================================

The market intelligence layer ("backend/opportunity_engine.py") combines empirical skill distributions from Analytics Jobs with role benchmarks from DataScience Jobs:

1. Role Capability Profiles:
   - For each role j, requirements are partitioned into:
     * Core Defining Competencies: skills where R_jk >= 0.50.
     * Secondary Competencies: skills where 0.15 <= R_jk < 0.50.

2. Compatibility Scoring Formula:
   For a candidate with capability vector C and experience E:
   - Core Skill Coverage:
     CoreCov = (Σ_k R_jk · min(C_k / R_jk, 1.0)) / (Σ_k R_jk) across core skills
   - Secondary Skill Coverage:
     SecCov = (Σ_k R_jk · min(C_k / R_jk, 1.0)) / (Σ_k R_jk) across secondary skills
   - Experience Alignment:
     ExpCov = min(1.0, E / max(ReqExp_j, 0.5))
   - Raw Weighted Score:
     RawScore_j = 0.55 · CoreCov + 0.25 · SecCov + 0.20 · ExpCov
   - Critical Core Deficit Penalty:
     If a core defining skill has R_jk >= 0.70 and C_k < 0.40, a non-linear deficit penalty is applied:
     RawScore_j = RawScore_j - (0.40 - C_k) · 0.45
   - Compatibility Score = round(max(0.0, RawScore_j), 3)

3. Role Status Categorization:
   - Reachable: Compatibility Score >= 0.65 (65%)
   - Stretch / Near-Reachable: 0.50 <= Compatibility Score < 0.65
   - Blocked: Compatibility Score < 0.50

The candidate capability model ingests evidence and creates a standardized state vector C in [0.0, 1.0]^10:

   - For each documented evidence artifact:
     * Source Strength Multiplier: STRONG (1.00 for Projects & GitHub), MEDIUM (0.85 for Coursework & Assessments), SUPPORTING (0.75 for Certifications), WEAK (0.60 for Self-declared).
     * Recency Half-Life: Inactivity decay factor = 2^(-RecencyMonths / 18.0).
     * Computed Capability Confidence = BaseScore × StrengthMultiplier × RecencyDecay.

3. Standardized Scale:
   - All capabilities are bounded between 0.00 (no demonstrated ability) and 1.00 (mastery).

10. BOTTLENECK DETECTION
==================================================

NEXUS does not select a bottleneck by simply finding the candidate's lowest proficiency score. A low score in an obscure or non-essential skill provides negligible transition leverage.

Implemented Bottleneck Scoring Formula:
For each canonical skill k, the system evaluates:
1. Target Role Gap: Average shortfall across all non-reachable (Stretch and Blocked) roles where skill k is required:
   Gap_k = (1 / |NonReachable|) Σ_{j ∈ NonReachable} max(0, R_jk - C_k)
2. Market Demand Weight: Relative overall frequency of skill k in the labor-market dataset:
   DemandWeight_k = Postings_k / max_m(Postings_m)
3. Counterfactual Opportunity Impact: Simulates an isolated +0.30 boost to skill k and computes how many previously non-reachable roles become reachable:
   ImpactScore_k = min(1.0, NewlyUnlockedRoles_k / 3.0)

Composite Transition Leverage Score:
PriorityScore_k = 0.35 · Gap_k + 0.35 · DemandWeight_k + 0.30 · ImpactScore_k

Skills are ranked in descending order of PriorityScore. The highest score identifies the exact capability change that provides the greatest transition leverage.

11. COUNTERFACTUAL / WHAT-IF SIMULATION
==================================================

The counterfactual simulation engine ("backend/simulation.py") is 100% deterministic and reproducible.

Flow:
Baseline Capability (C) + Experience (E)
↓
Select Preset Intervention (I) ∈ {A, B, C}
↓
Apply Capability Boost: C'_k = min(1.0, C_k + Boost_k × Intensity)
↓
Recalculate Role Compatibility for all 11 canonical roles
↓
Compute Opportunity Expansion & Newly Reachable Roles
↓
Generate Quantitative Causal Explanation

Preset Interventions Implemented (from "config/interventions.json"):
1. Intervention A: Advanced AI/ML & Deep Learning Specialization
   - Affected Skills: machine_learning (+0.35), python (+0.15), math_statistics (+0.15)
   - Rationale: 78% of ML Engineer and 43% of Data Scientist roles demand ML.
2. Intervention B: Analytics Translation & Executive Storytelling
   - Affected Skills: visualization_storytelling (+0.30), business_analysis (+0.25), sas (+0.20)
   - Rationale: JDS empirical finding proving storytelling and translation drive high salary hikes.
3. Intervention C: Production Data Engineering & Cloud MLOps
   - Affected Skills: big_data_engineering (+0.35), cloud_deployment_mlops (+0.35), sql_database (+0.15)
   - Rationale: Unlocks high-volume, high-paying engineering tracks (8,000+ vacancies, 11.8L–19.0L).

Opportunity Expansion Formula:
Opportunity Expansion(I) = (|Reachable Roles after| - |Reachable Roles before|) / max(|Reachable Roles before|, 1)

Safe zero-denominator handling: If baseline reachable roles = 0, expansion is 1.0 (100%) if reachable after > 0, else 0.0.

==================================================
17. LIMITATIONS
==================================================

1. Sample Size Constraints:
   - JDS (n = 139) and SDS (n = 161) datasets represent relatively small cohorts. While stratified cross-validation indicates strong internal consistency, models should not be generalized to universal psychological or workforce truths.

2. Correlation vs. Causation:
   - Observed trait relationships in SDS and skill correlations in JDS represent empirical associations within the supplied sample. Conscientiousness and storytelling skills are correlated with past high outcomes, but cannot be claimed as sole causal mechanisms.

3. Prototype Intervention Assumptions:
   - Capability improvements from interventions (+0.25 to +0.35) are prototype simulation parameters defined in "config/interventions.json". Real-world skill acquisition depends on individual learning velocity and educational quality.

4. Resume Keyword Heuristics:
   - Local resume parsing uses term frequency and keyword matching. It does not parse deep contextual nuances or semantic project impact.

5. Cross-Dataset Harmonization:
   - Analytics Jobs provides individual unstructured postings, while DataScience Jobs provides aggregated enterprise vacancy figures. While merged harmoniously for market modeling, they represent slightly different survey methodologies.
